Cake Order Desk: the business and its life cycle

A teaching example of a multi-agent system. Some agents are AI, some follow fixed rules, and at set points a person must approve before work goes on.

The business. A small neighbourhood bakery makes celebration cakes to order. Customers write in their own words, for example "chocolate cake for about 20 people, unicorn theme, one guest is gluten-free, Saturday please". Today the baker reads every message, works out a price, checks the calendar and writes back by hand. The Cake Order Desk gives that work to a team of agents. AI agents read the messy human input, rule agents do the sums and the scheduling, and the baker steps in only when an order really needs a human decision.

Every order moves through four stages, in a straight line. Each stage takes one input, produces one output, and hands that output to the next stage. Because the stages only talk through these hand-offs, each stage can be designed, built and tested on its own.

Customer the person ordering AI AI agent: reads or writes natural language Rules deterministic agent: same input, same output Human a person doing the work Human gate a person must approve before the flow continues

Simplification for this course: the flow only goes forward. Nothing loops back to an earlier stage. There is no rework, no change request after confirmation and no complaint handling. The only branch is in Stage 1, where the baker may decline an order, which ends it. Problems and loop-backs are a later topic.

3. Zoom in: Stage 1 Ordering (what iteration 1 builds)

Inside the stage, an orchestrator runs the agents one after another. Each agent reads what earlier agents wrote on the order and adds its own part. The orchestrator itself is rules only. It decides nothing clever, it just moves the order along and keeps the log.

Review rule: when does the baker look?

The order goes to the baker if any of these is true:

  • the design is custom (not a catalog cake)
  • there is a dietary or allergy need
  • the quote is over $150
  • Intake's confidence is below 0.8, or a field is missing
  • Capacity found no slot (the baker decides, usually to decline)

Otherwise the order is confirmed straight away, with no person involved.

Why AI here, rules there?
  • AI where the input is human language: understanding the request, and writing a brief a decorator can follow.
  • Rules where the answer must be exact and repeatable: money, calendar, routing.
  • Human where judgement and responsibility matter: can we really make this, and is it safe for the allergy?
  • The rule agents also check the AI. If Intake reads "20 people" as 2, the price and the review show it.
