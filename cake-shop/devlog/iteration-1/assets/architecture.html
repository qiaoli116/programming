Cake Order Desk: how the parts work together

Companion to devlog 07. The app as built after devlog 06: the parts, the two agents, and what happens between "send" and the cake changing on screen. The x-ray view in the app draws this same map, live.

1. The map

Four layers from left to right: the people, their browser pages, the server's front door (the API), and the server's back room (agents, rules and the store). The numbers follow one message, "actually it's only 10 people", and match the step list in section 3.

The one idea. The AI agents propose; the rules decide. Every change to an order, whether it comes from the panel or from the designer, passes the same check() and price() before it is saved. The agents never call each other directly either: the host writes a job on the board, and the board is read by the designer. The board is part of the order row, so the customer, the baker and the browser all see it.

Three loops, nested

  • The customer's loop (browser): send, show the answer, poll while busy. It never waits on the designer.
  • The host's turn (server, inside one request): up to 3 model steps. It ends with reply; a task becomes a queued job.
  • The designer's job (server, background, one at a time per order): up to 6 tool calls, then report. It works on a copy; only progress lines are saved until the commit at the end.
