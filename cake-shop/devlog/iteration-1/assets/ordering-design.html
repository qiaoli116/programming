Stage 1 Ordering: designing the cake together

Cake Order Desk · iteration 1 · goes with devlog 20261113-02-ordering-design-chat.md. Sample people, dates and prices are fictional. "Today" in this page is Fri 13 Nov 2026.

1. The idea

The customer designs the cake in a chat with an AI assistant. Every design is a template from our catalog plus option values, so anything that fits is something the bakery knows it can make. Like a real designer, the assistant shows possibilities: while the wish is still open, it offers a few options side by side, each with its own picture, price and designer's note, and the customer presses Choose on one. Then the two refine it together. On every turn the customer sees the picture, the price and what we can't do. When they are happy, they press Confirm design. The order then waits for the baker to approve it, and Ordering is done.

Before (devlog 01)

  • The customer sends one message and hears nothing until the end.
  • The AI reads free text and writes a free-text brief.
  • The baker sees only flagged orders and may edit them without asking the customer.

Now

  • The customer and the AI go back and forth until the design is right.
  • Designs are template + options, checked by rules on every turn.
  • When the wish is vague, the AI offers 2–4 options, each with a picture, price and note. The customer chooses one with a button.
  • The customer confirms. The baker approves or declines every order, without editing it.

The one rule to remember: AI for the conversation, rules for the promises. The AI understands the customer and suggests designs. But every design and price the customer sees has been checked or worked out by a rule agent. If the AI gets something wrong, the rules catch it before the customer sees it.

2. One turn of the conversation

Designing is live, not a queue. The customer and the AI talk on the left. In the middle is the current design. The customer can change it in two ways: by asking the AI ("make it purple"), or directly with quick changes in the design panel (a colour swatch, a tick box, a size menu). A quick change is written into the chat as a note, so it is in the AI's context, and the AI replies with a short confirmation ("I see you changed the colour to purple"). Whenever the design changes (the AI proposes or updates it, or the customer presses Choose or makes a quick change), the three rule agents run at the same moment: the checker says whether it can be made, Pricing adds up the total, and Preview draws the cake. Their results appear in the design panel beside the chat, so the customer sees the picture and price change while they are still talking. If the checker says no, that version is never shown, and the reasons go back to the AI, which proposes something else or explains the problem.

The AI may…

  • understand what the customer means ("about 20 kids" → serves 24)
  • choose the closest template and options
  • suggest changes and explain trade-offs
  • say no politely and offer an alternative

The AI may not…

  • invent an option. The design format has no field for "3D sculpted head"
  • state a price of its own. The reply uses the Pricing agent's number
  • decide that the customer has agreed. Only the Confirm button does that
  • approve an order. Only the baker does that

    4. The design catalog
    

    Four templates, each with a short list of options. Each picture below is drawn by the Preview agent from a sample design, the same way as in the chat. The catalog is a config file that the baker can change without changing any code.
    

    

    Never offered, whatever the template: 3D sculpted shapes, likenesses of real people, more than two tiers, orders with less than 3 days' notice, and delivery outside the local area. The AI knows this list so it can say no politely. The rules don't need it, because a design made of template + options cannot even describe these things.

    6. Stage 1 from start to end
    

    The chat is a loop, but it happens inside one status (DESIGNING). The stage as a whole still only moves forward: once a design is confirmed it is frozen, and nothing goes back to the chat.

    The rule from devlog 01, "one writer per column, written once", gets one exception: draft columns may change while the status is DESIGNING. Once the status moves on, every column is frozen. The old columns request, spec, brief, review and slot are dropped. The pickup date lives in design, and the chat replaces the request, and the design replaces the spec and the brief. Stage 2 reads design, a list of known options, which is easier for the kitchen to follow than free text.
    



    7. Open questions
    

    
  
    #QuestionProposed
    
  
    Q1Preview: layered pictures drawn by rules, or an AI-generated image?Layers drawn by rules, as on this page. They are exact and free, and what you see is what we make. An AI image could show things we can't make.
    
  
    Q2Who makes sure the kitchen has room on the day?The baker, when approving. There is no Capacity agent in Stage 1. The checker only enforces the minimum notice, and the baker declines if the day is full. An automatic capacity check could come back in a later iteration.
    
  
    Q3What happens to chats that just stop?After 7 days without a message → ABANDONED. This could also be left out of iteration 1.
    
  
    Q4Is a catalog of four templates enough for the demo?Yes: it is enough to show "can do" and "can't do" cases, while keeping the set of pictures small.
