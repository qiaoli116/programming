0 Design review: from the demo to the app

We reviewed the teaching demo (ordering-design.html) against the agreed decisions and the product catalog. The flow and the agents stay the same: AI for the conversation, rules for the promises, live checks, pricing and preview, quick changes the AI knows about, a Confirm button, and baker approval. This page is what a customer and a baker would actually use. The table lists what the final design adds or changes.

AreaIn the demoIn the final app
StartingThe customer types a first message.Start screen asks for the four facts the catalog needs first: occasion, number of guests, date and dietary needs. Free text is optional. The AI's first reply is a shortlist that already fits, not a guess.
TemplatesFour product lines named as templates.Real templates from the catalog (Unicorn round, Picture sheet…), suggested by occasion. The pictures are drawn by the same Preview rules.
Order factsOnly in the chat.Order details strip pinned above the design: occasion, guests, date, dietary. Tapping one changes it, and that change is noted in the chat like any quick change.
Live statusImplied by the Confirm button.A status badge on the picture: "✓ We can make this", always as text and icon, not just colour.
Quick changesPlain form controls.Named colour swatches, a segmented flavour control, decoration chips with prices, a gluten-free switch. Choices the cake can't take are disabled with a reason ("Red velvet isn't available gluten-free"). Every change is noted in the chat, and the AI confirms it.
Size vs guestsNot connected.The size control shows "serves N" and warns when it is fewer than the guests in the order details. The AI says the same.
ConfirmingOne button closes the chat.Review & confirm step: the frozen picture and spec, the price, pickup or delivery, contact details, and an explicit "this design is final" consent. No payment until the baker approves.
After confirmingA system message.An order status page with a timeline. The customer gets an email or text when the baker decides.
BakerA single inbox card.An approval inbox sorted by pickup date, a detail view with the rule checks, the orders already confirmed for that day (to help the kitchen check), the full chat including quick changes, and Approve or Decline with a reason. There is still no edit button.
ScreensOne demo page.Desktop first (1280 px and wider). Phones are not designed in iteration 1.

3 Review & confirm

Confirming is a deliberate step, not a chat message. The customer sees exactly what will be frozen and sent to the baker, adds pickup and contact details, and agrees that the design is final. Pressing the button sets the status to AWAITING_BAKER.

5 Baker approval

The baker sees only orders waiting for approval, soonest pickup first. Each one is already valid and priced, so the review is quick: the rule checks are ticked, and the one human check (does the kitchen have room?) is helped by the list of orders already confirmed for that day. Approve, or decline with a reason the customer will read. There is no edit button, by design.
